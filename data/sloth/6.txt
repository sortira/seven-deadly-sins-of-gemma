Silas had a brilliant idea for a novel, one that had lived in his head for a decade. He bought a beautiful leather notebook and a fountain pen, but they sat untouched on his nightstand, gathering a thick layer of grey silt. Every morning he promised himself he would write a single sentence, and every evening he watched the sun set with a dull, heavy sense of relief that he hadn't tried. He preferred the perfect, unwritten ghost of a book to the messy, tiring reality of creation.
