The banquet table was a mountain of roasted meats, dripping fats, and sugar-glazed pastries. Elias had already eaten until his belt bit deep into his stomach, but he reached for the honey-soaked pheasant anyway. He didn't taste the herbs or the smoke; he only felt the frantic, heavy need to fill the void. He shoved another handful of cake into his mouth, his breathing shallow and labored, terrified of the moment the plate would be empty and he would have to face the silence of his own hunger.
