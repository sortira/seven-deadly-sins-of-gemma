The chef stood over the burnt soufflé, his face a mask of terrifying stillness. The kitchen staff froze, the usual clatter of pans dying into a suffocating silence. He didn't yell. Instead, he picked up a porcelain plate and dropped it. Then another. He watched them shatter with a clinical satisfaction. Each crash was a punctuation mark in a silent lecture on incompetence. He wanted them to feel the same jagged ruin he felt in his soul every time his perfection was compromised.
